Menton and the villages above

Menton is in France, the last town on the coast before Italy, with no border checks from Monaco. It has a colourful old town climbing up from the harbour, long beaches, famous gardens, and medieval villages on the mountains behind it.

Getting there from Monaco

Trains from Monaco Monte-Carlo station reach Menton in about 10 minutes. The next stop, Menton-Garavan, is closer to the old town’s east side, the harbour and the Italian border. The coastal bus 600 also runs from Monaco to Menton, more slowly.

Taxis

Menton taxis are French taxis. They can take you to Monaco, but Monaco taxis can’t collect you in Menton, and Menton taxis can’t collect you in Monaco. The main ranks are outside Menton station, by the Casino, and at the town hall.

The old town and the basilica

Colourful lanes and stairways climb from the harbour to the Baroque Basilique Saint-Michel-Archange, with views over the sea. It is lovely, but steep and stepped, so it isn’t suitable for wheelchairs or pushchairs.

Keep climbing above the basilica to the Cimetière du Vieux-Château, on the site of the old castle. Its grand 19th-century tombs belong to the British, Russian and other winter visitors who made Menton famous, among them William Webb Ellis, said to have invented rugby. It also has the best view over the town: the photo at the top of this page was taken there. It is still in use, so please be quiet and respectful.

Down in the town you can still see the grand hotels built for those winter visitors. The Winter Palace on Avenue Riviera, built in 1901 with 220 rooms, became private apartments in the 1950s and is now a listed historic monument: admire it from the street.

Museums and gardens

  • Musée Jean Cocteau, Séverin Wunderman collection: Rudy Ricciotti’s striking white building by the seafront has been closed since storm damage in 2018. Part of the collection is on show at the Galerie des Musées in the Palais de l’Europe, by the Biovès gardens.
  • Musée Jean Cocteau, Le Bastion: Cocteau’s own small museum in a 17th-century fort on the harbour.
  • Palais Carnolès: the former summer palace of the princes of Monaco, now an art museum with a garden of citrus trees.
  • Jardins Biovès in the centre, and the Jardin botanique du Val Rahmeh in Garavan, full of tropical plants.

Les Halles, the covered market

Menton’s covered market fills a colourful Belle Époque hall at the foot of the old town, a few steps from the beach. Come in the morning for local cheese, olives, charcuterie and the town’s famous lemons.

The Lemon Festival

Every February, the Fête du Citron fills the Jardins Biovès with giant sculptures made from lemons and oranges. In 2027 it runs from 13 to 28 February, with parades of floats on the Promenade du Soleil on Sunday afternoons (14, 21 and 28 February). Trains to Menton are very busy on parade days.

Mirazur

Mauro Colagreco’s restaurant by the Italian border has three Michelin stars. Book well ahead.

The villages above Menton

Menton’s Zest buses leave from the bus station next to Menton train station: line 10 to Sainte-Agnès, one of the highest coastal villages in Europe at about 780 m (around 35 minutes), line 5 to Gorbio and line 6 to Castellar (around 30 to 35 minutes each). They run only a few times a day, so check the times before you go.
